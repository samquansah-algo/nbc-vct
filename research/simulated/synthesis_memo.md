# Synthesis memo: study 02 (SIMULATION)

> **SIMULATION. NOT MARKET VALIDATION.** This memo codes ten fictional interviews with fictional personas. Nothing here is customer evidence or a usable testimonial. Counts and percentages are computed by script from `coded_responses.json`; this memo gives none.

## 1. Method as applied

- All inputs and transcripts were read in full before coding.
- Codes follow `coding_scheme.md`: one code per persona per assumption, each with a verbatim quote from the persona's own answer in the named section. A script confirmed every quote is an exact substring of the stated section (whitespace normalised only).
- **Eligibility.** B1 is coded only for P01–P05; P06–P10 are *Not Assessable: outside buying authority*. B6 is Not Assessable for everyone. B3, B5 and B7 carry context codes only, and their status is Not assessable whatever the codes show.
- **Willingness to pay.** WTP is coded only against the approved C1 offer. Three of its terms remain **HYPOTHETICAL**: the 25% deposit, help within one working day, and exit with 4 weeks' notice. Non-buyers' (P06–P10) interest is recorded as recommendations, not WTP.
- **Doubt rule.** Where I hesitated between two codes, I took the less favourable one and wrote a `doubt_note`.

## 2. Notable coding judgements

- **B8 rule.** *Mixed* if a non-specialist partly succeeded or a guide would have sufficed; *Challenging* if they dropped the activity, needed more than a guide, or turnover was named as structural.
- **B2 rule.** Coded on the cohort named at C1 plus Q8 constraints; the unfavourable result is probably an artifact (§4).
- **B4.** One-day training that "changed nothing" was coded Challenging, since failing light development threatens the ≤$350 line. P08's learning-by-doing account is the main counterexample.
- **WTP boundary between Negotiate and Reject.** *Negotiate* means the persona accepts the price in principle and names conditions, usually proof, written terms or approval (P01, P03, P04, P05). *Reject* means the objection is the recurring price itself and the only way back is materially different terms. P02 is Reject: "Today, I would not pay the deposit" and a free first term. P03 was closest to Accept but attached conditions. For P05, Negotiate was kept because he says the deposit is "not the obstacle".
- **Objections.** O11 is coded only for P04 (partner) and P05 (board); P01 decides alone, and non-buyers' need for approval is structural.
- **Emergent objections (O14–O19):** device failure or in-session support gap; exposure from an early Show-Me Day; ownership of the parent relationship; content read as play or "babyish"; a closed system; one device for too many children.

## 3. What the simulation suggests (qualitative only)

- **B1 (price).** Among fictional buyers the per-child price was rarely the stated barrier at mid-fee schools and centres. Barriers were peer proof, written terms, approval and timing. The low-fee proprietor (P02) rejected the recurring price as more than a teacher's termly pay. Cash timing is contradictory: P01 says the start of term is when she has least in hand; P02, P03 and P05 call it the easiest time. **No payment is established.**
- **B8 (unaided delivery).** Every account describes practical computing that stops without "the one person". Several personas believe a step-by-step guide, a troubleshooting page and materials kept in school would be enough. Reluctant users (P06, P08) say one unsupported failure in front of children would end their use.
- **B9 (parents).** In schools, parents judge on exam results. Visible computing appears as a retention factor at transition years (P01, P05, P07). In the centres and for the parent persona, what the child can show or explain counts for more. Disconfirming cases show parents reading card-based or Scratch work as "just playing".
- **B2 (utilization).** Named pilot cohorts at centres, and at the low-fee school, fall well below 160. At mid-size schools they approach or exceed it (P05, P07).
- **B4 (development cost).** Personas report cheap past training with little effect, plus teacher turnover. Both imply re-onboarding costs.
- **B3, B5, B7 (context only).**
  - B3: continuation is tied to low management load, parent response and "fails twice" stop rules.
  - B5: buyers want peer visits and references, not demonstrations.
  - B7: marks dominate, although several teachers use informal transfer tests (change it and fix it; unseen questions).
- **Candidate unmet need.** Keeping hands-on computing running when the specialist is absent; competing readings are print resources alone, a staffing problem, or a Q4 wording artifact.

## 4. Artifacts and limits

- **Persona construction.** Backstories were written around faded programmes and single points of failure. The transcripts reproduce this almost uniformly, and C1 stances track the assigned attitudes.
- **Prompt wording.** Q4 presupposes a single specialist. The C1 offer text is echoed back as written-term demands. Asking "how many children would take part" produced pilot scopes, not annual capacity, which weakens B2.
- **Offer arithmetic.** The flat $250 discount produced $0 and $20 first terms (P06, P08).
- **Shared model tendencies.** Phrasings recur across countries ("salaries first", "in front of the children", failing "quietly", prize-giving days, "honestly"); they are model-generated language, not market vocabulary. Balanced "marks count, but…" answers make B7 uniformly Mixed.
- **Not independent.** All personas come from one model family, so agreement across them is not corroboration. One or two personas per country support no country-level inference.
- **Cannot establish:** payment, conversion, churn, setup time, unaided delivery, validity or transfer. Priority fieldwork (in the JSON) starts with the Step 21 deposit test and Wizard-of-Oz observation.
