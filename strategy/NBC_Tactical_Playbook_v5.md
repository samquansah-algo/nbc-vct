# NBC Tactical Playbook
**Revision v5 | 25 September 2026 | Planning window: 25 September–20 November 2026**

## Venture introduction

NBC develops learning infrastructure for children growing up with AI. We combine programmable physical experiences with evidence of what children can explain, create and do when a task changes. Building from Algo Peers’ practice in Ghana, we are testing how this approach can work across homes, schools and community learning environments worldwide.

Current stage: an operating Algo Peers practice supports a new NBC research and product hypothesis. The first commercial offer is a proposed supported Learning Worlds programme. Existing programmes, customers and the LMS provide access; NBC purchases and outcomes must be tracked separately.

## The class milestone slide: five connected elements

| Element | Slide-ready answer |
|---|---|
| Current Evidence | Algo Peers has delivered hands-on learning, built an LMS and documented learner interactions. These provide practitioner knowledge and access. NBC’s proposed combination of reusable worlds, evidence and independent delivery is ready for feasibility testing. |
| Next Choices | Develop a reusable learning kit; provide a supported programme; add evidence tools where they improve instruction; or redesign the experience before expansion. |
| Gating Uncertainty | Can children learn something they can use in an unfamiliar task when an external facilitator delivers the experience, and can an operator sustain its total delivery cost? |
| Next Milestone | Complete a bounded paid pilot with baseline and delayed unfamiliar tasks, independent scoring, repeated delivery by two external facilitators, complete cost logs and documented buyer continuation decisions. |
| If Met / If Missed | If the evidence is promising, replicate in a contrasting setting and design a stronger comparative learning study. If it fails, identify whether learning design, measurement, delivery or purchasing caused the failure, then revise that component before expansion. |

The milestone is a decision checkpoint. A completed prototype or workshop alone does not meet it. A small pilot provides feasibility and early evidence, not a causal effectiveness claim.

## Weekly submission structure

1. **Venture introduction:** elevator pitch, current offer and evidence stage.
2. **Tactical playbook:** the five elements above, updated to the week’s decision.
3. **Experiment demonstration:** dated instruments, task photographs without unnecessary identifying data, workflow examples, offer versions, anonymized scoring or cost records.
4. **Results and interpretation:** actual denominator, failures, missing observations, what changed and what remains unresolved.
5. **Venture updates:** customer, team, research and product developments, explicitly separated from proposed activity.

Use additional evidence slides when they carry information. Do not fill them with synthetic screenshots or invented results.

## Workstream 1: the decision and buyer

**Question:** What recurring learning problem is important enough for an operator to change a programme or pay for support?

Proposed recruitment: 12–16 exploratory conversations across operators, facilitators and parents, including people outside existing friendly relationships. Recruit comparable-age learning settings in Ghana and, if accessible, one context with extensive AI access.

### Interview guide

1. Walk me through the last practical learning session you ran.
2. Tell me about the last time a child completed an activity but you were unsure what they understood.
3. What did you do next? What did that take?
4. Show me how you currently record progress or explain it to a family.
5. Describe a time a learner used an idea successfully in a different activity.
6. What makes practical sessions difficult to repeat with another facilitator?
7. What have you paid for to improve this programme? How was that decision made?
8. Which current tools or routines work well enough that you would keep them?
9. After discussing the problem, present the scoped pilot and ask: What would have to happen for this to fit your programme?
10. What is the actual process for allocating money, approving delivery and agreeing a start date?

Capture actual examples before presenting NBC. Record refusals and reasons. A compliment is not a buying signal.

**Evidence fields:** participant role; setting; warm/new relationship; recent problem; workaround; consequence; frequency; buyer; budget process; existing spend; offer response; next concrete step.

**Decision:** choose one recurring programme problem and one buyer. If stories point to different problems, segment them before averaging responses.

## Workstream 2: experience design and learning

**Candidate capability:** designing an informative test by changing one factor while controlling relevant alternatives. This is a candidate, subject to age and domain review.

Create a one-page specification:
- What the child needs to know beforehand.
- What actions and explanations would support the intended interpretation.
- What success could occur through guessing, imitation or external help.
- What language, motor or sensory demands could obscure understanding.
- What assistance and accommodations are permitted.
- What changes between the familiar and unfamiliar tasks.
- What evidence would challenge the construct or interpretation.

Build three meaningful contexts with inexpensive materials. The first may support learning; later tasks examine whether the child can apply the idea when relevant features change. Do not assume that a new story alone establishes transfer.

Have a suitable learning specialist and domain-informed reviewer inspect the tasks. Small usability sessions establish whether instructions and actions are interpretable. They do not establish effectiveness.

**Data fields:** task version; prior experience; baseline; assistance; actions; explanation; revision; immediate response; delayed unfamiliar task; missingness and reason.

**Decision:** retain, adapt or replace the experience. If children enjoy it but cannot explain or use the intended idea, revise the learning design before improving the dashboard.

## Workstream 3: minimum useful evidence

Compare:
- A short facilitator probe and observation.
- A structured event record.
- Automated capture only after a specific manual bottleneck is found.

Have assessors score a subset independently and without seeing each other’s scores. Prespecify the agreement metric and acceptable interpretation after rubric review and before collecting the main results.

Ask whether the richer record changes a next-step decision appropriately. If it supplies more data without changing a useful decision, simplify.

**Avoid:** using repeated interactions as independent learners; training and evaluating on the same child/task records; treating rater agreement as causal evidence; inferring individual capability from group work alone.

## Workstream 4: independent delivery

Recruit two facilitators outside the founding delivery team. Each runs repeated sessions using the same documented materials.

Log preparation time, delivery time, support contacts, founder interventions, participation, equipment failure, interpretation questions and accessibility issues. Record support even when supplied informally by message.

**Decision:** a supported programme may be viable even when a self-service kit is not. Price the actual support model rather than assuming the founder’s assistance disappears.

## Workstream 5: paid demand and unit economics

Offer the same clearly scoped pilot to 10 qualified buyers, while recording justified variations. Proposed continuation gate: three meaningful paid commitments. This is a resource-allocation rule for a small pilot, not an estimate of population demand.

An offer must specify:
- Participants and session scope.
- Materials and ownership or return terms.
- Facilitator preparation and support allowance.
- Evidence summary supplied.
- Price, payment timing, cancellation and refund terms.
- Responsibilities and delivery dates.

Price from a full cost sheet. Do not use old model outputs as a market-validated quote.

**Per-delivery cost log:** consumables; facilitator hours; support hours; transport; compute; energy; repairs; equipment allocation; evidence review; payment fees; refunds. Track cash and fully loaded costs separately.

**Buyer evidence:** money collected; programme activated; benefit reported with an example; continuation requested; renewal paid when due. Use remains important even after payment.

A direct-to-family monthly LMS offer is a separate experiment through existing families. Test repeated value, adult support burden, materials and actual renewal before expanding acquisition.

## Workstream 6: contrasting contexts

The first contrast should retain a similar age range and intended construct while varying one consequential condition, such as access to AI or the delivery setting.

Recruit a research partner in a contrasting context. International location alone is not a meaningful comparison. If no partner is ready, replicate locally and keep broader claims open.

Record adaptations before comparing results. Preserve necessary assistive technology. Optional AI assistance can be varied only when that variation fits the research question and participants’ needs.

**Decision:** adaptation may demonstrate good design, not failure. The question is whether the same capability claim remains justified after the adaptation.

## Workstream 7: intelligence-utility option

During this cycle, document utility opportunities without creating a second sales operation. Test an additional workload only if:
1. A specific user identifies useful demand and a budget.
2. Existing capacity can support it without degrading learning access.
3. Incremental labour, energy, quality assurance and maintenance are measured.
4. Revenue can be separated from the learning account.

A promising utility opportunity earns a separate experiment. It does not rescue an otherwise unsupported learning business case.

| Prior belief | Hypothesis and confidence before results |
| Instrument/version | Exact task, script, offer or cost sheet |
| Actual evidence | Counts, dates, denominators and source artefacts |
| Alternative explanation | What else could produce this result? |
| Decision | Continue, revise, simplify, defer or stop |
| Next trigger | What evidence would change the decision again? |

## Eight-week closeout

Report learning, evidence quality, independent delivery, demand and economics separately. Possible outcomes include:
- Promising learning and delivery with paid continuation: replicate, measure renewal and design a comparative study.
- Useful learning but excessive support: simplify or sell a transparently staffed service.
- Strong engagement with unclear learning: improve task design and assessment before efficacy claims.
- Good measurement with weak activity design: improve the experience; do not prematurely pivot to child scoring.
- Learning value but weak payment: investigate payer or sponsor fit with an explicit subsidy model.
- Simple tools outperform the proposed infrastructure: retain the useful experience and remove unnecessary complexity.
- Insufficient recruitment or precision: report an unresolved question and decide whether further evidence is worth the cost.

**Global ambition remains constant. The product, claims and investment change with evidence.**
