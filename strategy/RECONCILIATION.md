*Inputs:*
- [`NBC_Thesis_and_Strategy_v5.md`](NBC_Thesis_and_Strategy_v5.md) and [`NBC_Tactical_Playbook_v5.md`](NBC_Tactical_Playbook_v5.md): drafted with ChatGPT, provided by the founder, stored verbatim (checksums match the uploads);
- the repository's latest analysis, [`foresight/NBC_HCG_STRATEGY_2026.md`](../forecasting/NBC_HCG_STRATEGY_2026.md), with the v4 edits of DEV-019/020.

*Purpose:* say objectively where v5 is stronger, where the repository adds something v5 lacks, and which differences only the founder can resolve. **Nothing in the repository has been switched to v5 wholesale.** The corrections listed in §3 have been applied. Everything else waits for the decisions in §4.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Recommendation: adopt v5 as the operating plan for the next eight weeks, with the six amendments in §3.**

v5 is more focused and more realistic about what an eight-week cycle can establish:
- it frames that cycle as **feasibility and decision research**, not a validity result;
- it puts learning before measurement;
- it makes **independent delivery by external facilitators** a gate.

The repository's work remains useful in three places v5 leaves thin:
- the cost of credible evidence (task sampling);
- ethics and data protection for research with children;
- the benchmark of external institutions.

The two documents mostly agree. The real disagreements are in §4.

## 2. Where v5 is stronger (accept)

| # | v5 position | Repository position | Why v5 is better |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Learning before measurement.** Four separate propositions: engagement, capability development, interpretable evidence, sustainable delivery (v5 §1) | Centred on evidence validity (§8 V1) | An attractive activity, a reliable assessment and an effective intervention are different achievements. The repo's plan could reach a validity result for an experience that isn't worth doing |
| 2 | **Candidate capability: control of variables** ("designing an informative test by changing one factor", Playbook WS2) | Proportional reasoning, debugging, planning (unselected) | CVS has direct transfer evidence at this age. Chen & Klahr (1999; N = 87, ages 7–10) found children learned and transferred it, including to remote contexts, **with explicit instruction plus probes, not probes alone**. It's a well-grounded first construct |
| 3 | **Independent delivery gate:** two external facilitators, full support logs (Playbook WS4) | B8 in the business model, but missing from the HCG 8-week plan | Tests whether NBC works without the founding team, which is the core of "institutional portability" |
| 4 | **A meaningful contrast, not just geography:** "International location alone is not a meaningful comparison". Vary one consequential condition, e.g. **AI access** (a US child with extensive AI vs a Cape Coast child) | "≥2 countries", with Kenya/Nigeria/India as candidates | Two scarce-access sites test replication but not the thesis's claim about AI abundance. v5's abundance-vs-scarcity contrast tests it directly |
| 5 | **Held-out assessment tasks.** Show-Me Day materials can't double as research tests (v5 §6) | Marketing §5 put a "transfer moment" into Show-Me Day | Rehearsed or circulated tasks contaminate the delayed transfer test. **Corrected in the marketing strategy** |
| 6 | **Separate accounts** (learning / intelligence service / creator services); the downside case has zero utility revenue (v5 §5) | Staged economics in thesis §XVIII | Cleaner, and it stops utility revenue from rescuing a weak learning case |
| 7 | **Old prices and model outputs are historical;** price from a full cost sheet and pilot logs (v5 §5) | Same principle; models labelled as assumptions | Consistent. v5 states it more firmly for the pricing sheet |
| 8 | **Forecast register F1–F5** with resolution rules | Forecasts F1–F8 in the four-methods analysis | v5's forecasts are narrower, resolvable and tied to decisions |
| 9 | **The family LMS subscription is a separate experiment,** not run alongside sales to programme owners | Not covered | New channel information; kept separate as v5 advises |
| 10 | **Assistive technology is part of valid participation.** An "unassisted" condition must not remove it | "Assistance state" field; AI removed for a CDTC | An important correction to the repo's CDTC definition. **Applied (§3 A5)** |

## 3. Where the repository adds what v5 lacks (amend v5 with these)

| # | Amendment | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Measurement gate must include task sampling, not only rater agreement.** Report how many tasks a capability claim rests on, and estimate task-to-task variance (a G-study) | Shavelson, Baxter & Gao 1993; `hcg_economics.py` | Rater disagreement is usually the *small* error source in performance assessment. Task sampling dominates, so a claim from one or two tasks is weak however well raters agree. v5's measurement gate names only agreement |
| A2 | **Week 0: ethics review, parental consent and child assent, and a data-protection plan** before any data collection. Include the jurisdiction rules for a US contrast site | HCG strategy §8.2; Ghana Data Protection Act 2012 (Act 843) | v5 is silent on ethics approval. Research with children in two countries will need it, and it takes time. **It may push the delayed-task weeks past week 8.** Plan for that honestly |
| A3 | **Instruction + probes, not discovery alone.** Worlds must include explicit CVS instruction and probe questions | Chen & Klahr 1999 | Probes without instruction didn't improve CVS in that study. "Programmable experiences" alone may not teach it |
| A4 | **Keep a simple-alternative comparison** (facilitator probe or portfolio) as a rival to richer evidence | HCG strategy S6; v5 Playbook WS3 | Already in v5 ("compare simple probe with richer evidence"). Both documents agree, so keep it explicit |
| A5 | **Revise the CDTC definition:** "AI assistance removed" becomes "optional AI assistance removed; necessary assistive technology retained and recorded" | v5 §4 | Fairness; applied to the definition used in the HCG strategy |
| A6 | **Evidence-cost model:** keep `hcg_economics.py` for the *cost of credibility*. Don't use it for pricing, which comes from v5's cost sheet and pilot logs | Both | They answer different questions |

## 4. Real disagreements: founder decisions

| # | Question | v5 says | Repository (DEV-019/020) says | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **What is the headline thesis?** | "Learning infrastructure for the next billion children". The HCG is "a technical and research concept, not the primary parent-facing sales promise" and "could" support learning "over time" | Founder, 25 Sep: "**The Human Capability Graph is the broader thesis**" | **Layer them.** For children and parents and in all public language: learning infrastructure. For investors, researchers and funders: the HCG is the long-term thesis the learning work generates evidence for. They conflict only if one document has to serve every audience |
| **D2** | **Age band for the first task family** | ~9–12 | ~9–15 | **9–12** for the first CVS task family (closest to the Chen & Klahr evidence). 13–15 is the next band |
| **D3** | **Youth TVET / RPL (15–24) as the alternative first payer** | Not in the plan. Employment recognition is "outside the initial commercial plan" | Named alternative (HCG strategy §5.2) | **Defer** to an option after this cycle. It conflicts with a 9–12 first task family and would split an eight-week plan |
| **D4** | **Industry clusters and a Capability Evidence Council** | Not mentioned. "Domain-informed reviewer" of tasks only | First wave (electrical/energy; ICT) as co-designers; council after M2 | **Defer and narrow.** Keep the §11 benchmark as background. In this cycle, use **one domain-informed reviewer** for the CVS tasks (v5 WS2). Move SSB/SSAC conversations (D2, I2 in the HCG plan) and the council to after the first cycle, when a 13+ or TVET task family is in scope. *This reverses part of the DEV-020 recommendation: added to an eight-week plan already at capacity, it was more breadth than the evidence supports* |
| **D5** | **Scenario axes** | Availability of reliable affordable intelligence × institutional acceptance of contextual evidence | Premium on unassisted capability × control of recognition | **Either works.** v5's first axis is close to "fast vs slow AI", but framed as *access in children's settings*, which is genuinely uneven. Keep both sets, since they stress different choices |
| **D6** | **The contrasting site** | A comparable-age site with substantially different AI or resource access; the US is illustrative; access isn't established | A second country from the community directory | **Adopt v5's criterion.** Recruiting a high-AI-access site is a recruitment objective, and ethics approval there (A2) is on the critical path |
| **D7** | **"200 paying customers historically"** | Stated in v5 §10 as the founder's latest conversational figure | Not in any repo source | **Verify before use.** Record the period, the product and what "customer" means (family, site). It isn't an NBC figure |
