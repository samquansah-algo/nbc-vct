# Economics and forecasts

NBC has no revenue yet. This page covers market size, how we will make money, how a site breaks even, scenario projections, and the forecasts we are tracking. **Projections here follow arithmetically from stated assumptions. They are not forecasts, and we will rebuild them from pilot data.** All figures are in US dollars.

## Market size

| | Size | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | $29–53B a year | Global after-school programme market, 2025. Estimates from several research firms range from $28.6B to $52.75B because definitions differ. For scale, UNESCO reports 1.4 billion students enrolled worldwide (2024) |
| **SAM** | ~$99M a year | Ghana: about 18,530 primary and 8,850 junior high institutions (2020 sector data), roughly 27,400 sites × $3,600 a year |
| **SOM, year 5** | ~$3.2M a year | 800 paying sites in the central scenario, about 3% of Ghana's schools |

Site price: 240 participant-years × $15, the thesis's own illustration. At that volume, direct cost is $2,640 a site ($1,200 fixed plus $6 per participant), leaving $960 of contribution per site. Many public schools won't pay directly; sponsors and public partners fund those sites (see [PARTNERSHIPS.md](PARTNERSHIPS.md)).

## One core business, two options

| Account | Who pays | What has to be true first |
|---|---|---|
| **Core: learning service** | Programme owners pay a programme or site fee. Parents may fund it through fees. A family subscription through our learning platform is a separate test | Paid delivery, a credible learning result, repeat use |
| **Option: shared AI service** | Programme owners or communities pay for useful AI work, run on shared infrastructure | Repeat paid demand, and a positive contribution after all extra costs |
| **Option: creators and licensing** | Buyers of licensed experience packages, set-up services, and later verification | Useful, rights-cleared content, real demand, and sound evidence wherever verification is sold |

- **The learning business must stand alone.** Our downside case assumes zero revenue from both options.
- **What we leave out of the business case:** selling children's data (never), assumed royalties, tokens and speculative marketplace income.
- **AI evaluation environments** remain a research question, not a revenue plan.
- **Public-private partnerships** fund access where families can't pay; see [PARTNERSHIPS.md](PARTNERSHIPS.md).

## How a site breaks even (illustration)

With a site price **P**, fixed delivery cost **F** and variable cost per participant **V**, break-even is **F ÷ (P − V)** participants, as long as P is higher than V and the site has capacity.

| Participants a year | Cost per participant |
|---|---|
| 60 | $26 |
| 120 | $16 |
| 240 | $11 |

This example assumes $1,200 fixed delivery cost a year and $6 per participant. At a collected price of $15, a site covers these costs at **134 participants**. **This is an illustration, not our price or forecast.** Real prices come from a full cost sheet built during the pilot, covering materials, facilitator time, logistics, AI and energy, maintenance and evidence review.

## Forecasts we are tracking

Each forecast is narrow, has a date or condition, and a rule for deciding whether it came true.

| # | Forecast | How we'll know | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | At least one of our first three paying programme owners will value facilitator support or the experience itself more than the evidence record when deciding to renew | Written renewal reasons; unresolved if fewer than three renewals | We sell today's learning value first, and fund the evidence record by how useful it proves |
| F2 | By 30 September 2027, an AI model of comparable quality will cut our measured AI cost from its starting baseline | A fixed workload and quality bar set in the first 30 days, then re-tested | We keep AI providers replaceable and never count on savings in advance |
| F3 | At least one task instruction or facilitation step will need adapting at our first contrasting site | Changes logged before comparing outcomes | We invest in clear task definitions and adaptation records |
| F4 | Buyers will ask for evidence of programme benefit before paying separately for portable recognition of skills | Actual offers, decisions and contracts | We hold off pricing credentials until there is demand |
| F5 | For the next 24 months, the core learning business will need to work with zero revenue from the options | Compared against actual collected revenue | We keep a stand-alone cost case for learning |

## Projections: three scenarios

| Year | Low: sites | Low: revenue | Central: sites | Central: revenue | High: sites | High: revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | $11K | 5 | $18K | 10 | $36K |
| 2 | 10 | $36K | 30 | $108K | 60 | $216K |
| 3 | 25 | $90K | 120 | $475K | 300 | $1.35M |
| 4 | 50 | $180K | 350 | $1.39M | 900 | $4.05M |
| 5 | 100 | $360K | 800 | $3.17M | 2,500 | $11.25M |

**How the numbers are built:**
- **Core revenue:** paying sites × $3,600 a year.
- **Options:** the options (shared AI service, licensing) add 0% in the low case, 10% in the central case and 25% in the high case, from year 3. The low case assumes none, as the thesis requires.
- **Second country:** the high case adds a second country from year 3.
- **Site contribution in year 5:** $96K (low), $768K (central) and $2.4M (high), before shared costs.
- **Data:** full numbers are in [projections_scenarios.csv](projections_scenarios.csv).

**What moves the numbers most:**
- how many programme owners pay after the pilot;
- the price actually collected;
- how many renew;
- facilitator time per site;
- how much sponsored access public partners fund.

We will replace these assumptions with pilot data and re-publish, with the changes shown.
