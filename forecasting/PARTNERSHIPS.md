# Pathways for public-private partnerships

NBC's first customers are programme owners who can pay. Many children can't pay, and most of them are in public schools. Partnerships let public and private money reach those children without the core business depending on grants.

*These are the routes we are pursuing. Each one needs a named partner, a budget and a signed agreement before we count it. None is a partnership today unless stated.*

## Two entities, one mission

| Entity | Role |
|---|---|
| **Algo Peers Ltd** (Ghana, commercial) | Builds and sells NBC: Learning Worlds, facilitator support, the shared AI service |
| **Algo Peers Impacts** (Ghana, non-profit, company limited by guarantee) | Brings the same learning to public schools and families who can't pay, funded by grants, sponsors and public partners |

The product and the teaching are the same in both. Paying customers fund product development; sponsors and public partners fund access. We never assume fees from better-off families can cover every public school.

## Six pathways

| # | Pathway | Who does what | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sponsored public-school access** | A company or foundation funds Learning Worlds in public schools. Algo Peers Impacts delivers them with the local education office's approval. Algo Peers Ltd supplies the product at a published price | First sponsored sites alongside the programme owner pilot |
| 2 | **Curriculum and teacher development with education authorities** | As computing becomes compulsory in Ghana's schools, education authorities license Learning Worlds and facilitator training for their teachers | After the pilot shows children learn and teachers can deliver |
| 3 | **Shared community learning centres** | Libraries, community centres and makerspaces host kits and a shared AI for the neighbourhood. Local government provides the space, private sponsors fund equipment, and NBC runs the programme | After one centre runs a full term |
| 4 | **University research partnerships** | Universities co-design and independently evaluate the learning studies, starting with Ghanaian universities | From the first comparative study |
| 5 | **Technology partners, in kind** | Hardware, cloud and AI companies contribute devices, credits or tools. Past in-kind support includes hardware from the Micro:bit Educational Foundation | Ongoing |
| 6 | **Pay-for-results financing** | Funders or governments pay when children reach agreed, independently verified learning results | Only once our evidence is strong enough to verify results |

## Rules for every partnership

- **Children's data is never part of the deal.** No partner receives children's records or pays for them.
- **Adults stay responsible.** Children are reached only through their schools, programmes and families.
- **Sponsor money is ring-fenced.** It is budgeted per site, reported separately, and never used to hide losses in the core business.
- **Evidence comes before scale.** We sign a scaling agreement only after a site shows learning, delivery by outside facilitators, and a sustainable cost.
- **Written or it doesn't count.** We don't list an institution as a partner without a signed agreement.
