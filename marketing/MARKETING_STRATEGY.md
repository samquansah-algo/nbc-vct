## 2. Brand architecture (a decision for the founder)

Three names are live: **NBC**, **Algo Peers**, and **Papa Algo**. Using all three undifferentiated will confuse buyers. **Recommended:**

| Brand | Role | Audience | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NBC** | The **infrastructure and institutional brand**: the Node, the standard, the licence, the network | Funders, governments, partners, investors, licensees | Proposals, licences, evidence reports, the node itself ("NBC Node") |
| **Papa Algo** | The **child-facing character** who guides children through the worlds | Children, parents | Worlds, passport, Show-Me Day, storybooks |
| **Algo Peers** | The **Ghana operating and delivery partner**, with its existing trust, network and after-school channel | Proprietors, operators, parents in Ghana | Sales, delivery, afterschool.algopeers.com, local contracts |

**Customer-facing line in Ghana:** *"Papa Algo learning worlds, powered by the NBC Node, delivered by Algo Peers."*

**Why:** local buyers trust a known local operator, children connect to a character, and funders fund infrastructure. **Decide before printing any materials.** The entity question (whether NBC becomes a separate company) is separate and waits for the pilot results.

## 5. The Show-Me Day engine

Show-Me Day does three jobs at once: **it is the product's evidence protocol, the retention event for paying sites, and the acquisition event for prospects.**

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| When | Once per term at each paying site; prospects invited to the nearest one |
| Format | 45 minutes. Children in small groups demonstrate a capability in a world, then try a **changed demonstration task** (a public transfer moment). Parents watch; a witness stamps passports. **v5 correction:** Show-Me Day uses **demonstration tasks only**. The held-out tasks used to assess delayed transfer never appear at Show-Me Days or in marketing, because rehearsed or circulated tasks contaminate the evidence ([reconciliation](../strategy/RECONCILIATION.md) §2 row 5) |
| Who attends | Parents; the proprietor or operator; 3–6 invited prospect proprietors; occasionally a district officer or sponsor |
| What prospects take away | A one-page "What our children showed this term" (aggregate, no names); a pilot offer sheet with the all-in price |
| Follow-up | Field lead visits within 7 days; pilot agreement signed on the second visit |
| Guardrails | Written parental consent for any photo or name; no ranking of children; every child shows something; no data about children in marketing |

**Funnel it feeds** [MODEL]: contacted → attends Show-Me Day → discounted pilot term → pays term 2 → renews → refers.

## 7. Pricing communication

- **Lead with the termly all-in number per site**, not per child. Show the per-child figure as a comparison with fees and lab costs.
- **Say out loud what's included:** node, solar, materials, worlds, teacher development, Show-Me Days, reports, repairs and replacement.
- **Discounted first term** with a written return commitment. The synthetic panel says trust is earned by coming back, not by the launch [SYNTHETIC].
- **Instalments per term.** Never lump sum.
- **Sponsored access** is presented separately to sponsors, never as a discount negotiated by poorer schools.
- **Never** make a child's participation depend on a parent top-up.

## 9. Trust, safeguarding and reputation

Trust is the moat and the biggest marketing risk. Before any public marketing:
1. **A safeguarding dossier:** DPIA, consent and assent forms in local languages, data-flow diagram, deletion policy, incident process.
2. **A marketing consent policy:** written parental consent for images or names; first names only; no school or location details that identify a child.
3. **A claims register:** every public claim linked to its source and verification status (following the claim-check table in the Algo Peers Navab package).
4. **An evidence standard:** learning claims only after the validity study; until then, market engagement and demonstrations and label them as such.

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Overclaiming learning before evidence | Claims register; market demonstrations, not outcomes, until the validity study reports |
| Show-Me Day becomes a performance or ranking | Every child shows something; no rankings; the transfer moment is about the group, not individuals |
| Parents feel pressured to pay | Parent money only through the school or centre; sponsored places available |
| Founder-dependent selling | Hire and train a field lead by Q2; association and referral channels from Q2–Q3 |
| Brand confusion | Decide the brand architecture in week 1 |
| A safeguarding incident | Dossier before launch; consent policy; incident process |
| *v4* Drift into employer screening of young people | Mission guard: no selection use of under-18 records; employer products need an explicit founder decision (HCG strategy §5.1) |
| *v4* Borrowed prestige (implying WEF or council endorsement) | Claims register covers affiliations; name global bodies only as sources |
| *v4* Cluster sprawl | Two clusters in the first wave; second wave only after M2 |
