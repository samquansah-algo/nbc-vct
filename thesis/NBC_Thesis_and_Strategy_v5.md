## 1. The venture thesis

### Core thesis

**NBC is developing learning infrastructure for the next billion children growing up alongside increasingly capable AI. It combines programmable physical experiences, machine intelligence and evidence from meaningful action to help children develop and demonstrate capabilities they can carry into unfamiliar situations.**

Its ambition spans countries and learning environments. It begins with Algo Peers’ practice in Ghana and tests its principles across contrasting contexts. Over time, these experiences could support a Human Capability Graph that represents what a learner has demonstrated, under which conditions, with what assistance and with what uncertainty.

### The problem and the opportunity

Our strategic premise is that access to useful machine intelligence will continue to expand, although unevenly. Explanations, generated content and completed outputs may become easier to obtain. The opportunities through which children exercise judgment, investigate consequences, create, collaborate, revise and become capable remain costly in time, materials, skilled attention and institutional support.

The problem appears in different forms. A child may lack equipment and meaningful opportunities to experiment. Another may have extensive AI access yet struggle to explain or reproduce a result when assistance changes. A learner may demonstrate understanding through action that an existing assessment fails to recognize. These are hypotheses about distinct needs to investigate, not a claim that all children experience the same deficit.

NBC’s opportunity is to make high-quality learning experiences more accessible, adaptable and economically repeatable, while improving the evidence used to decide what a child should experience next.

**Central question:** What infrastructure helps children turn access to intelligence into capabilities they can exercise with agency, extend through practice and demonstrate when circumstances change?

### Learning before measurement

The experience must be worthwhile for the child. Curiosity, purposeful action, feedback, explanation and revision are central. Evidence collection supports these experiences and the decisions around them.

NBC must test four separate propositions:
1. Children can meaningfully engage with the experience.
2. The experience helps develop a defined capability.
3. Its evidence supports a useful, appropriately bounded interpretation.
4. The experience and evidence can be delivered sustainably by people beyond the founding team.

An attractive activity, reliable assessment and effective learning intervention are different achievements. Each requires its own evidence.

Capability includes reasoning, making, collaboration, care, inquiry, adaptation and participation. Economic opportunity matters, alongside agency and a fulfilling life. Foundational knowledge is part of capability: transfer depends on what learners know about the new situation.

### Global scope with a focused beginning

The mission concerns children across technology-scarce and technology-rich environments. Relevant settings include homes, schools, libraries, museums, community programmes, after-school centres and makerspaces.

The recommended initial commercial segment is **independent programme owners of recurring hands-on learning programmes for children aged approximately 9–12**, beginning with reachable programme owners in Cape Coast. This age band is a proposed design choice for the first task family, not NBC’s permanent age boundary. Recruitment and task review should determine its suitability.

A contrasting research site should serve a comparable age group with substantially different access to AI or learning resources. It may be in Ghana or another country. International access is a recruitment objective, not an existing partnership.

The architecture may eventually support learning across life stages. Entering adult markets would require a separate mission and investment decision, domain evidence and customer discovery.

### Short elevator pitch

NBC develops learning infrastructure for children growing up with AI. We combine programmable physical experiences with evidence of what children can explain, create and do when a task changes. Building from Algo Peers’ practice in Ghana, we are testing how this approach can work across homes, schools and community learning environments worldwide.

## 2. Architecture and the role of each component

| Component | Purpose | First implementation | Evidence required before expansion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Computational matter | Give objects and environments changeable roles, rules and responses so the same materials support different learning experiences | Reusable objects, printed tokens and a clearly specified rule system; instrument only a concrete need | Added feedback, accessibility or reuse justifies complexity and cost |
| Experience Protocol | Describe objects, states, actions, consequences, assistance and evidence consistently | A human-readable experience specification and event schema | Another facilitator can execute it; different interfaces preserve intended task meaning |
| Learning Worlds | Provide challenges with consequential choices, feedback and revision | Three deliberately different worlds around one bounded capability family | Feasibility, child experience, delayed performance and appropriate comparison |
| Educator intelligence | Make useful instructional routines easier to learn and use | Preparation guides, worked examples, interpretation prompts and human support | External facilitators use them successfully; support burden is affordable |
| Intelligence layer | Support creation, translation, preparation and bounded feedback | Existing tools plus human review; local, cloud or hybrid according to task | Quality, latency, access and full cost beat a simpler alternative |
| Human Capability Graph | Connect tasks, contexts, observations and cautiously inferred capability | Small evidence model; an ordinary database is sufficient initially | Relationships improve a real learning decision beyond a simple record or portfolio |
| Utility and contribution layer | Share infrastructure costs and reward useful experience creation or services | Cost allocation and creator agreements for any actual contributions | Paying demand, acceptable governance and positive incremental contribution |

**Institutional portability:** NBC seeks to make valuable learning routines easier to reproduce without requiring every site to recreate an expensive institution. Human relationships, local judgment, care and governance remain essential operating inputs.

**Deployment:** A hub, node, shared computer, ordinary materials or existing devices can be implementation choices. NBC does not commit to hardware ownership or a specific sensor stack before repair, reliability and financing implications are measured.

### Human Capability Graph: bounded meaning

A node may represent a defined capability, task, context or observation. An edge may represent “observed in,” “requires,” “supported by” or a tested relationship between tasks. Relationship types must have explicit semantics; similarity is not proof of transfer.

Records separate observed performance, inferred capability, predicted performance and causal learning effects. They retain relevant assistance, collaboration, language, accommodations, task version, provenance and recency. A group success does not establish individual mastery.

The first useful decision is likely to be which challenge or support to offer next. Portable recognition and credentials are later uses requiring independent acceptance. No general intelligence score, permanent child ranking or inference of adult employability belongs in the initial product.

Families and learners need age-appropriate explanation and ways to correct records. Data retention should follow a specific purpose. Marketing participation is separate from assessment. The child’s record is not the revenue product.

## 3. First offer, buyer and market sequence

**Working offer: NBC Learning Worlds.** A supported sequence of reusable physical learning experiences, facilitator guidance and a concise evidence summary that helps choose the next learning step.

Initial user: children in recurring programmes. Daily programme owner: a facilitator. Buyer: the programme owner or budget holder. Parents may fund the programme owner through fees. Sponsored access requires an identified sponsor and explicit delivery budget.

Algo Peers’ existing family-paid programmes are an access and pricing research channel. Historical Algo Peers customers must remain distinct from NBC purchasers.

Compare the family route cheaply through existing relationships, then choose according to paid repeat use, acquisition cost, support and learning opportunity. The presence of an LMS does not establish subscription demand.

## 4. Global research design and personas

## 5. Economics: the learning business and the utility option

### What creates economic value

The first economic hypothesis is that reusable experiences and useful facilitator support can improve the quality or reach of a learning programme at a cost its buyer can sustain. Evidence may add value by improving the next learning decision and communication with families.

Machine intelligence is one input. Its availability does not remove physical materials, skilled attention, preparation, maintenance, safety, distribution or validation costs. Count these explicitly.

### Three separate accounts

| Account | Possible payer/revenue | Required gate |
|---|---|---|
| Core learning service | Programme owner fee, family subscription, separately funded sponsored delivery | Paid delivery, credible learning proposition and repeat use |
| Shared intelligence service | Contracted useful workloads for programme owners or communities | Repeat paid demand and positive contribution after all incremental costs |
| Creator and infrastructure services | Licensed experience packages, implementation or later verification services | Useful rights-cleared content, buyer demand and sound evidence where verification is sold |

A fourth possibility, AI evaluation environments, remains a research option. Its demand, technical requirements and governance are untested. Exclude child-data sales, assumed royalties, tokens and speculative marketplace proceeds from the core business case.

An intelligence utility could lower shared costs and enable local participation in value creation. Its advantage must be demonstrated against existing accessible services. Protect learning time and capacity when allocating shared resources.

### Full cost model

Use one consistent period and currency, preserving local-currency inputs and the dated exchange-rate assumption.

- Net collected revenue = fees actually collected less refunds and applicable transaction deductions.
- Direct delivery cost = materials consumed + facilitator and support time + logistics + compute and energy + maintenance + evidence review.
- Site contribution = net collected revenue − direct delivery cost.
- Operating result = total site contribution − shared product, administration, research and other operating expense.
- Cash runway includes equipment purchases, deposits, replacement reserves, payment delays and financing separately from depreciation.
- Acquisition cost includes founder time at a disclosed replacement rate, travel and failed prospects. Report cash spend and fully loaded cost separately.
- Cost per learner served, cost per completed experience and cost per successful delayed task are different measures.
- Cost per additional learner reaching a defined delayed-performance threshold requires a credible comparison. Do not calculate causal cost-effectiveness from raw completion counts.

Where site price is P, fixed delivery cost is F and variable cost per site is V, a simplified operating break-even is F / (P − V), provided P > V and capacity remains sufficient. Replace the simplified formula when staffing increases in steps.

**Illustration only:** annual fixed delivery cost of $1,200 and variable cost of $6 per participant-year imply a specified cost of $26 at 60 participants, $16 at 120 and $11 at 240. At a hypothetical collected price of $15, 134 participants cover those specified costs. This excludes any costs not included in the assumptions and is not NBC’s price or forecast.

### Pricing and financing decisions

Quote a bounded pilot after building a cost sheet. Test what the buyer is paying for: a stronger programme, reusable activities, reduced preparation or useful evidence. Do not force a per-child price if a site licence or programme fee better matches value and administration.

The prior $4/$6/$2.50 termly prices, $7.02 cost, $866 acquisition cost and LTV estimates remain historical model assumptions. They do not establish current unit economics. Rebuild the model from pilot logs before publishing projections.

Maintain downside, base and upside cases with explicit inputs for paid sites, collected price, renewal, facilitator time, maintenance and utilization. The downside case has zero utility or contribution revenue. Higher utilization counts as beneficial only while quality, access and staffing remain acceptable.

Use research support to finance measurement uncertainty and early product development where available. Earned revenue tests demand. Large production commitments and a valuation target should follow evidence about product, delivery and repeat purchasing.

## 6. Next milestone and eight-week tactics

**Central milestone:** Establish whether one NBC experience sequence can be delivered independently, produce interpretable evidence of children’s learning on an unfamiliar task, and attract paid continuation at a support cost the buyer and venture can sustain.

The first cycle is feasibility and decision research. It prepares a stronger causal study; it does not establish broad efficacy.

| Element | Current operating definition |
|---|---|
| Current evidence | Algo Peers has delivered hands-on learning, runs an LMS and has relationships with families and institutions. NBC’s integrated architecture and new revenue model remain untested. |
| Next choices | A reusable experience product; a supported programme; an evidence feature within either; or redesign before expansion. |
| Gating uncertainty | Can children benefit from the experience when external facilitators deliver it, and does the benefit justify the delivery and evidence burden to a buyer? |
| Next milestone evidence | Baseline and delayed unfamiliar tasks; independently scored explanations/actions; external delivery logs; actual priced commitments; full cost and buyer-use records. |
| If met | Repeat in a contrasting setting, test renewal and design a powered comparative study. |
| If missed | Diagnose task, learning design, evidence, delivery or payer failure separately; simplify or change the relevant component before increasing production. |

### Proposed eight-week programme

| Week | Work and evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 initial programme owner/facilitator conversations and recent workflow examples; specify one learning problem and candidate age band | Which repeated learning decision and buyer to serve |
| 2 | Complete 12–16 discovery conversations; independent review of one capability specification and three task designs; construct costed pilot offer | Select one offer and a bounded learning claim |
| 3 | Small task-usability sessions; baseline probe; observation and scoring trial; begin offers to 10 qualified buyers | Repair task/accessibility problems; choose minimum useful capture |
| 4 | External facilitator delivery; record preparation, assistance, failures and child explanations | Self-service toolkit, supported programme or redesign |
| 5 | Delayed unfamiliar task; blind scoring; compare simple probe with richer evidence | Does added capture justify its burden? |
| 6 | Repeat delivery and document payments, buyer decisions and cost; confirm contrasting-site access | Continue paid pilot and select adaptations |
| 7 | Comparable feasibility work in contrasting setting if access is ready; otherwise complete local replication and keep global claims open | Which features travel and which require adaptation |
| 8 | Review all gates, customer continuation decisions and study design | Repeat, simplify, change payer or pause a component |

Recruitment targets are proposed, not sample-size justification. A learning scientist should set the participant sample and analysis for each research question before recruitment. Small usability sessions cannot resolve causal effectiveness.

### Experiment rules

Separate experience development from assessment validation and intervention efficacy. Use different or appropriately counterbalanced task forms to reduce practice effects. Predetermine what counts as useful improvement before outcomes are seen.

For later efficacy work, compare against a credible alternative, assess relevant baseline knowledge, use delayed unfamiliar tasks, blind scoring where feasible, and account for how groups are formed, clustering, missingness and facilitator differences. A changed surface alone may not establish meaningful transfer.

Maintain separate demonstration materials and held-out assessment tasks. Public Show-Me Days illustrate learning; they cannot simultaneously serve as uncontaminated research tests once tasks are rehearsed or circulated.

Avoid automated routing until a fixed sequence and human interpretation establish a useful baseline. Assess the added value of sensors and AI separately from the activity itself.

### Decision thresholds to lock before testing

| Gate | Proposed operating rule | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Paid demand | 3 of 10 qualified, independently approached buyers make a meaningful paid commitment on disclosed terms | Enough to justify a small pilot; not a market conversion estimate |
| Delivery | At least two external facilitators each run repeated sessions; support fits the prequoted allowance | Evidence of feasible delivery in those conditions |
| Learning feasibility | Baseline and delayed results, explanations and failure patterns are interpretable; no success claim from engagement alone | Advance to a properly designed comparative study |
| Measurement | Independent assessors meet a prespecified agreement criterion, with uncertainty reported; richer capture changes a useful decision | Reliability supports interpretation but does not establish validity |
| Economics | Repeated delivery has positive direct contribution under observed staffing, with full operating costs still shown | Initial delivery viability, not total company profitability |
| Cross-context use | Comparable-age tasks work sufficiently to interpret outcomes at a contrasting site | Justifies broader testing, not a universal claim |

If a statistic or threshold is not yet defensible, preregister it after task review and before testing. Do not retroactively choose a passing value. Record “inconclusive” when data cannot resolve the question.

## 8. Forecasts, projections and scenarios

### Four forms of future reasoning

- Projection: an arithmetic consequence of stated assumptions.
- Forecast: a time-bounded claim whose outcome can be evaluated.
- Normative vision: a future NBC prefers according to declared values.
- Scenario: a coherent possible environment used to stress-test choices.

The preferred future is one where children can develop agency and capability across learning settings, with meaningful access and understandable, contestable evidence. It is not an adoption forecast.

These are provisional judgments to register and update. Confidence is qualitative; no synthetic probability precision is claimed.

### Projections

Replace the previous automatic 3 → 6 → 12 → 20-site plan with a gate-dependent model. Model low, central and high values for actual collection, renewal, delivery capacity and support; keep assumptions editable. Show scenario results only after there is a defensible input sheet.

Forecast learning demand separately from computation prices. Cheaper AI may increase competition and raise expectations while lowering one operating cost.

### Scenarios with children at the centre

Axes: availability of reliable affordable intelligence, and institutional acceptance of contextual learning evidence. Measurement validity remains NBC’s responsibility under every scenario.

| Scenario | Child/family experience | Buyer and NBC response |
|---|---|---|
| Abundant intelligence, open recognition | A US child and Cape Coast child access different experiences but can share meaningful evidence; a multilingual learner retains context | Programme owners buy useful experiences; interoperable records gain value if recipient decisions improve |
| Abundant intelligence, fragmented recognition | Families can generate impressive outputs; schools and programmes still demand their own demonstrations | Provide learning experiences and local decision support; treat external recognition as optional |
| Constrained intelligence, open recognition | Shared access at a library or community centre matters; records travel more readily than equipment | Test shared service economics, resilient delivery and accessible evidence |
| Constrained intelligence, fragmented recognition | A rural family and community facilitator rely on trusted local demonstrations | Prioritize simple reusable materials, facilitator development and locally useful records |

**Substitution scenario:** ordinary materials plus expert facilitation achieve equivalent learning and decisions more cheaply. NBC must improve the experience, support or distribution enough to justify itself, or simplify to the useful component.

**Wild card:** automated capability scoring loses institutional trust. Human-readable observations, purpose limits, correction and a graph-free operating mode become important.

Review quarterly: actual buyer behavior, learning evidence, staffing cost, available model quality/cost, recognition requirements and relevant access constraints. Record what changes a decision, not only interesting news.
